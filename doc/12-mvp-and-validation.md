# MVP and Validation

## Goal

Do not begin by building the complete platform.

The first objective is to validate the economic behavior.

## Concierge MVP

Use:

- a simple web page;
- GitHub;
- Discord or similar communication;
- a spreadsheet/database;
- manual credit accounting.

No MCP.

No sandbox.

No autonomous agents.

## Recruit

Target:

### Builders

10–20 open-source maintainers or indie developers.

### Contributors

20–50 developers.

## Narrow initial scope

Example:

> TypeScript/React open-source tasks under two hours.

Task categories:

- tests;
- documentation;
- UI fixes;
- small bugs;
- dependency upgrades.

## Experiment

Give each participant a starting credit balance.

Builders publish tasks.

Contributors claim tasks.

The platform manually handles:

- task review;
- reputation;
- disputes;
- credit settlement.

## Questions

### Supply

Will contributors complete tasks without cash?

### Demand

Will builders trust strangers with tasks?

### Economics

What reward attracts contributors?

### Quality

What percentage is accepted first try?

### Liquidity

How quickly does a task receive a qualified claim?

### Credit utility

What do contributors want to spend credits on?

## Kill criteria

Consider stopping or pivoting if:

- contributors only participate when cash is offered;
- builders refuse to delegate even public tasks;
- review cost exceeds task value;
- credits accumulate but are not spent;
- task quality is consistently poor;
- the best contributors leave because rewards are insufficient.

## Success signals

Promising early signals:

- 50%+ of qualified tasks receive a claim quickly;
- 60%+ of claimed tasks are completed;
- 70%+ of completed tasks are accepted;
- contributors return after earning credits;
- builders publish additional tasks;
- credits are spent rather than hoarded.

These are hypotheses, not universal benchmarks.

## Next step

Only after the concierge experiment works should the team build:

1.  automated GitHub integration;
2.  credit ledger;
3.  reputation;
4.  task matching;
5.  MCP;
6.  sandboxing.
